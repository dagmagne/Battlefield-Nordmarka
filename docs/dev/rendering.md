# Rendering

`main.js` as orchestrator, HDR pipeline, terrain clipmap, snow mask, trees and tree felling, shadows, particles, thermal sight. Part of the developer notes indexed in `CLAUDE.md`; update this file when the system changes.

- **`main.js`** is the orchestrator: quality tiers (low/medium/high/ultra, auto-selected plus a manual override in the menu), pipelines, the frame loop, cameras, vehicle physics (`simulate`), fire control (`updateAim`), stamps, particles, dynamic resolution (DRS) and the perf harness.
- **Rendering:** forward HDR in `rgba16float` with 4× MSAA, reversed-Z infinite projection, a bloom chain, ACES tonemapping and dither. DRS renders into a sub-rect, so render targets are never reallocated.
- **`terrain.wgsl`:** geometry clipmap with 9 levels and CDLOD morphing. The heightmap is 2048² `r32float` at 2 m per texel (world 4096 m, playable area ±1000 m).
- **Snow mask (from the grooming mask):** maskA is `rgba8` (groomed, track, imprint, scorch) and maskB is `rg16float` (lateral u, longitudinal phase). The ski trails are pre-stamped as groomed at start; the tank only stamps its two track imprints. Shell impacts stamp round scorch marks into maskA alpha with a second pipeline (`fsBlast`), which `terrain.wgsl` turns into soot, thrown earth and a shallow crater.
- **Trees (`trees.js`, `tree.wgsl`, `treedeform.wgsl`):** about 207k instances on the high tier, 3 species and 4 LODs (37/180/850 m × `lodMul`), with a two-phase cross-fade. Snow load must look the same on every LOD (see "Lessons"). Per-tree variation lives in `treedeform.wgsl` as a function of the unit-height position and a hash of the trunk position, so every LOD and the shadow passes agree: a lopsided, wavy crown outline, lean, width, a bare lower trunk on big spruces (crown base `cb`; the dead branches below it are not drawn), a darker crown interior (`d.ao`), and the snow load and roughly metre-sized snow clumps, evaluated per vertex (`sv.x`; a fragment-shader noise for the clumps cost 3–5 ms on the GTX 1060). The mid and far spruce and pine models use irregular whorls (`tier(..., jit, js)`). The per-LOD snow extents (`SNOWX`, `?sx=`) were rematched with `tools/lodcmp.sh`, which now works in the real world, has snowfall turned off and needs no ffmpeg (`tools/imgmean.py`). The lodcmp frame mixes LODs (background trees dominate the mean), so read it as a check on background LOD pairs. The menu's Trees setting (`setTreeQ` in `main.js`; `localStorage.gtrees`, `?trees=high|medium|low|off`, `?notrees`) switches at run time. High is the full reference. Medium casts shadows from the mid and far models instead of the 4002-triangle spruce and scales the LOD ranges by 0.8. Low also draws a random half of the farthest trees from a thinned instance buffer (1.32× wider), scales the ranges by 0.55 and drops tree shadows from the far cascade. Off: not drawn or shadowed, not solid, no canopy shade on the snow (`lod2.y`), and no cover for enemy line of sight. Measured in the forest at Frognerseteren (all cascades drawn): 12.8 M tree triangles on high, of which 9.0 M in the shadow passes; about 5.1 M on medium and under 2.8 M on low. The debug overlay prints the live counts (`R.triStat`). On a weak Windows laptop GPU the frame rate was the same on high, medium and low (12–13 fps, 45 with trees off), so there the cost is per pixel (shading, overdraw, MSAA), not per triangle; tree cells are therefore drawn nearest-first, and `?tfs=0` draws trees in a flat colour to separate shading cost from fill cost. That laptop turned out to be rendering on its integrated Intel GPU (`gen-12lp`), not its NVIDIA card: 164 ms GPU time on high with 12 M tree triangles, 130 ms with flat-shaded trees, 37 ms on the low tier with 1.9 M, so it is mostly geometry-bound after all. The overlay and menu now show the adapter (`R.gpuName`), and the automatic tier drops to low on Intel `gen-N` adapters and medium on other Intel `lp` ones. On Windows the browser, not the page, decides which GPU a tab gets (Settings → System → Display → Graphics).
- **Shadows:** 3 cascades (the far ones update on alternate frames), PCSS on cascade 0, plus a baked heightfield shadow that is re-baked when the time of day changes.
- **Particles (`particles.js`, `particle.wgsl`):** kinds 0 chunk (gravity, lit), 1 smoke, 2 lamp glow (night only), 3 flash glow, 4 fire that cools into smoke, 5 screening smoke (looks like 1, but opaque to the thermal sight). Only kinds 0, 1, 4 and 5 live in the CPU pool; glows are written per frame.
- **Thermal sight (V: off, white-hot, black-hot; gunner sight and drone only):** when `G.groom.w` is 1 every scene shader returns an apparent temperature through `thermal()` in `common.wgsl` instead of lit colour (snow 0.1, forest 0.2, houses 0.24, hull 0.4, running gear 0.8, engine deck and fire above 1; vehicles have the engine at the rear, local +z). `thermalView` in `post.wgsl` turns that into the display (coarse detector grid, noise, blooming through the bloom chain; green for the tank, grey for the drone). Haze, dust and exhaust smoke are nearly transparent to it; smoke-grenade clouds are particle kind 5 (rendered like kind 1) and show as a hot, opaque aerosol. `?therm=1|2` forces it in every camera for test renders.

## Gotchas
- WebGPU doesn't allow `alphaToCoverageEnabled` together with a `sample_mask` fragment output. Use alpha cross-fades with alpha-to-coverage instead.
- Tree LODs have to match in mean colour and snow distribution. Simple cones with full snow cover read as white blobs and pop when they switch. Check with `tools/lodcmp.sh`.
- Wide LOD fade bands and extra skirt geometry cost about 35% GPU time. Keep the band at about 7%.
- First run on an NVIDIA card (D3D12) showed a line of flickering black and white single pixels with bloom halos in the snow. Suspected cause: the clipmap rings met on an exact shared edge on one side, and rounding opened pixel cracks to the background. The rings now overlap by a cell on every side (`mkIdx`), and `post.wgsl` clamps HDR values so a NaN or inf pixel cannot flash. The overlap did not cure it: with the clamp the pixels are black without halos, and they are reported to sit at the same screen positions however the view is turned, on NVIDIA only (fine on Intel, Apple and software WebGPU). Particles, glow sprites, lamps and the vehicle, drone and tree meshes were scanned and hold no non-finite values. `?hide=` (t terrain, s sky, p particles, v vehicles, o props, h shadow casters, x sight overlay) leaves parts of the frame out to bisect it on the card. Bisecting showed the line survived hiding any single part, stayed put when only the hull turned and moved with the camera: it comes from `skyBase` (used by `applyFog` on every surface), where `pow(az, 3.0)` got a base a rounding error below zero for view directions exactly opposite the sun's azimuth. `pow` of a negative base is NaN in HLSL; other backends return 0. Clamp the base of every `pow`.
- Object materials all have some specular, so a "black" surface in direct sun renders mid-grey. Use `MAT.void` for openings that must stay dark.
- Props that sit on the ground get darkened by the `hAbove` contact term per height texel, which shows as a 2 m staircase on large flat faces; flat snow faces (`MAT.snow`, facing up) skip it.
- The grooming mask covers `W.MW` metres (3200 real, 2048 fantasy) at 4096 texels; `stamp.wgsl` has the half-size as the literal `1600.0`, which `main.js` replaces per world. The far-terrain height (`262.0` in `terrain.wgsl`) and far ground plane (`255.0` in `sky.wgsl`) are replaced the same way for the real world.
- `object.wgsl` reads the ground height under a fragment bilinearly (for the contact term, `terrainShadow` and lamp light). With the nearest 2 m texel, surfaces lying just above a slope (stand treads) showed light and dark 2 m squares that looked like faceted geometry; no measurable GPU cost on the GTX 1060.
- **Trees and vehicles (`treeHit` in `main.js`, shared by the player and the enemies via `C.treeHit`):** a vehicle is a capsule (centre, forward, half-length, radius). Trees under `SMALL_T` = 7 m that the hull overlaps while it moves are knocked over: `fell()` aims the instance's rotation along the path and animates the fraction of its species field (`i1.y`, 0.004 → 0.4 over 0.7 s, one 4-byte `writeBuffer` per falling tree), which `treedeform.wgsl` turns into a tip-over about the base (lifted a little so the crown sits on the snow); a snow puff and a crack. Each vehicle has its own height limit (`treeHit(..., small)`): the Leopard 10 m; `ETYPE.fell` T-72 10, BMP 9, BTR 8, Tigr 6, buggy 4, bike 3. An enemy pressing against a big tree (`e.tcT`) fells trees up to 16 m after 1.5 s and anything after 3 s if heavy (`fell` ≥ 8), while a light one then squeezes through; the same after its first / second stuck event. Measured with `wavetest --waves 6`: about 6–10 stuck events per run against 3–4 before (2–3 with trees off), all resolved by the reverse/sidestep logic (0 problems); fewer would need real path finding round the forest. Bigger trees are solid: the vehicle is pushed out and slides along (enemies get a wall contact normal, like buildings); enemies also steer away from big trunks just ahead (`e.tax/taz`), run 35 % slower in full canopy (wheeled 55 %), and their overwatch, scoot and escape spots avoid dense forest (`okSpot`, canopy < 110/255). A restart stands the felled trees up again (`treesRestore`, up to 4096 remembered; past that a small tree stays solid; falls in progress use a ring of 64; a cell with a felled tree gets its culling bounds padded by the tree's length so a crown fallen over the edge is still drawn). Trees off: none of this. The thinned far-LOD copy on the low tier does not show felled trees (they are far away). `tools/treetest.mjs` checks it.
- The real world's tree count is capped (`cap` in `world.js`). If the cap is hit, the southern rows silently get no trees; check `__info()` (about 362k on high).
